Two datasets, one source: how Finnish electricity consumption data changes after publication

Daily captures of three Fingrid open-data datasets, 2026-08-28 to 2026-10-07.

By Joni Harju. Text prepared with AI assistance.

We captured three datasets from Fingrid's open-data service on 38 days between 2026-08-28 and 2026-10-07 and compared each captured hour with its earlier captures. Of the hourly consumption values in datasets 358 and 359 that we captured at least twice, 91.7–100 % per series changed after the first capture. Values still changed between day 21 and day 35 after the hour, the last age we capture. Both dataset descriptions say the consumption figures are derived from metering data that distribution system operators provide to Datahub, and the hourly totals of the two datasets agree to within 0.03 %. One can look like confirmation of the other, but by the publisher's own description both rest on one body of metering data.

2. The changes keep coming

The descriptions of datasets 358 and 359 say that estimated metering data is "generally corrected within 11 days". Yet every hour that we captured at both day 14 and day 35 had a different value at day 35 than at day 14. For 336 of these hours, day 14 was the first capture. Between day 21 and day 35, every hour changed in AB01, AB02, E13 and E14, and 65.2 % of the hours of E16 changed. We captured no hour after day 35, so we do not know when the changes stop. We do not know why they happen. The sentence is about estimated data, and later changes may have other causes.

3. Two datasets, one source

Dataset 358 splits hourly consumption by customer type. Dataset 359 splits it by metering method. Both descriptions say the data is derived from metering data that distribution system operators provide to Datahub. The classification comes from different data: customer types supplied by electricity suppliers for 358, and metering methods supplied by the operators for 359. If both rest on the same metering data, their hourly totals should agree closely, and they do.

Two datasets that agree this closely are not two independent confirmations of anything. By the publisher's description, they are one body of metering data, classified two ways. They are not identical: the totals never matched exactly, and the counts of accounting points differ. We have not shown what causes these gaps, and we have not shown that the two datasets are computed the same way. Both totals changed in every one of the consecutive capture pairs we compared, but that alone proves nothing, because the totals changed every time. (Some smaller categories of dataset 359 did not change at every step; see section 2.) The evidence for a shared source is the agreement of the totals and the publisher's own description.

4. A dataset that did not change

Dataset 400 (mFRR scheduled activation price, real-time) was captured at ages 1 and 7 days. Of its 15-minute values, 2,783 were captured twice, and none of them changed. Another 1,632 values were captured only once. Fingrid's description says that the final price of each 15-minute period is published 15 minutes after the period ends, and every capture we made came later than that. The same capture process left these stable values unchanged. That argues against the capture itself producing the changes in 358 and 359, but it does not tell what does.

What this report does not claim

  • It does not explain why values change.
  • It does not say that Fingrid or Fingrid Datahub did anything wrong. The dataset descriptions themselves say that estimated metering data is corrected after publication.
  • It does not make forecasts.

Appendix: method and limits

  • Capture. Daily from the Fingrid open-data API, datasets 358, 359 and 400, usually at about 16:05 UTC; captures on 2026-09-02 fell between 07:05 and 07:06 UTC and captures on 2026-09-10 fell between 16:05 and 17:05 UTC. The schedule captures each target hour of 358 and 359 at ages 5, 6, 7, 10, 14, 21 and 35 days, and no other ages occur. Because the capture window is 41 days long, only 96 hours per series were captured at all seven ages. Dataset 400 was captured at ages 1 and 7 days. Raw responses were not stored. Values were stored as published and compared as exact decimals.
  • Coverage. 38 capture days out of 41 (2026-08-28 to 2026-10-07). No capture on 2026-09-01, 2026-09-04, 2026-09-21. Two captures on 2026-09-10; the later one is used. The 1,872 values present in both captures of 2026-09-10 were identical. Capture started on 2026-08-28, so the earliest target hours were first seen at an older age.
  • One dataset only. On some capture days a target day was present in only one of 358 and 359: only 358: 2026-08-18 (captured 2026-08-28, age 10), 2026-08-22 (captured 2026-08-28, age 6), 2026-08-16 (captured 2026-08-30, age 14), 2026-08-31 (captured 2026-09-05, age 5) and 2026-09-04 (captured 2026-09-18, age 14); only 359: 2026-08-19 (captured 2026-08-29, age 10), 2026-08-10 (captured 2026-08-31, age 21), 2026-08-18 (captured 2026-09-08, age 21) and 2026-09-12 (captured 2026-09-18, age 6). Comparisons between the totals of 358 and 359 use only hour-and-capture pairs in which both datasets have all their categories.
  • Daily sampling. A value that changed and changed back within a day is invisible.
  • Frozen data. This report uses the first 71,566 lines of the capture file (sha256 7155501bf1800ed41a30320bf4fc27c2135ae0e93a3155989d3b8ba535f62fd1, last capture 2026-10-07T16:06:20.166206Z). The file is append-only, so this prefix does not change. All numbers are recomputed from it by one script.
  • Not used. No data or results from the separate forecast experiment that runs on the same capture.
